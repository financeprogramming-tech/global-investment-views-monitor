# Global Investment Views Monitor

## Overview

The Global Investment Views Monitor is a semi-automated generative AI workflow designed to transform fragmented public market information into a structured weekly investment intelligence brief.

The project combines a fixed source map, a structured Google Sheets database, a Google Docs reporting template, ChatGPT scheduled tasks and Make automation. The objective is not to produce investment advice, trading signals or portfolio recommendations. The objective is to support recurring market intelligence by monitoring public macro news, institutional investment views, consensus, divergences, narrative shifts and key risks.

## Project Rationale

Investment information is abundant, but institutional market views are difficult to compare, trace and monitor over time. Banks, private banks, asset managers and CIO teams publish regular outlooks, commentaries and market updates, but these publications differ in format, frequency, vocabulary, horizon and level of conviction.

The project was built to explore how generative AI can support the first layer of investment research: source monitoring, information structuring, cross-asset synthesis and professional report drafting. The large language model is used as an analytical assistant, not as an autonomous investment decision-maker.

## Problem Addressed

The central problem addressed by the project is not information access, but information comparability, traceability and repeatability.

A simple market summary can describe what happened during the week. However, it does not necessarily distinguish between macro news, official institutional views and analytical interpretation. The Global Investment Views Monitor addresses this gap by using a controlled source universe, structured analytical fields and a validation process before distribution.

## Workflow Architecture

The workflow follows a controlled sequence:

1. Source map definition
2. Scheduled AI-assisted source review
3. Google Sheets database update
4. Google Docs draft report generation
5. Human validation
6. Transfer to the ready-to-send folder
7. Make-based PDF conversion, archiving and email distribution
8. Transfer of the original document to the sent folder

Make is used only after human validation. It does not perform investment analysis. Its role is limited to document conversion, PDF archiving and controlled distribution.

## Source Framework

The source universe is organised into three layers:

- **Layer 1: Macro and market media**  
  Used to describe the macro and market backdrop, index movements, policy events and market reactions.

- **Layer 2: Official institutional investment publications**  
  Used to identify official investment views, consensus, divergences and cross-asset positioning signals.

- **Layer 3: CIO and strategist qualitative signals**  
  Used to enrich qualitative interpretation when available and relevant. These signals are not treated as formal house views unless explicitly published as official institutional positions.

## Quality Controls

The workflow includes several controls designed to make the output more reliable and auditable:

- fixed source universe;
- distinction between market context and institutional views;
- separation between evidence and analytical interpretation;
- source freshness checks;
- market data validation;
- standardised classification of views, conviction and horizon;
- explicit flagging of missing or inaccessible sources;
- human validation before distribution;
- Make automation activated only after approval.

Each institutional view must be linked to an approved source. Macro and market media sources can be used to describe the market backdrop, but they cannot be presented as official institutional investment views.

## Limitations

The workflow relies on publicly accessible and technically available sources. It does not represent internal institutional research, actual portfolio positioning or client-specific recommendations.

The fixed source universe improves consistency and traceability, but it also creates coverage bias. The consensus identified by the workflow reflects the selected institutions only and should not be interpreted as a complete measure of market-wide positioning.

The project is a market intelligence workflow. It is not an investment decision-making system, a forecasting model or a portfolio construction tool.
